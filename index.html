Investigating the Epistemological Friction of AI in Music

CHAPTER 01

The Paradox of Simulated Emotion

"If a machine mimics a scream, is there any pain in the sound?"

Art has traditionally served as a bridge between two human souls. My investigation explores the boundary between machine computation and human empathy: if AI triggers a genuine emotional response, does the 'falseness' of the source invalidate the 'truth' of the feeling? I argue that while the stimulus is algorithmic, the epistemological weight remains with the human listener.

CHAPTER 02

The Metadata of Morality

Generative AI is a mirror of cultural history, yet its outputs are often presented as "ownerless." I propose the AI Music Nutrition Label: a framework to de-anonymize training data.

By tracing the lineage of digital motifs back to the 'Data Donors'—the human artists whose legacies power these models—we shift the focus from Product-based value to Lineage-based ethics.

CHAPTER 03

The Curator's Manifesto

"Does art require labor to be valuable?"

In an age of infinite generation, 'Genius' is being redefined. We are transitioning from the era of the Executor (the player) to the era of the Curator (the thinker). While the machine provides the efficiency, only the human provides the intentionality that transforms sound into music. My heart remains in the work.
